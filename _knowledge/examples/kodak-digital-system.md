# Kodak: Optimizing the Wrong System

## The System

An imaging market contains a coupled system: film manufacturing, camera hardware, software/digital processing, customer behaviour, and business models. All feedback into each other.

## What Happened

**Kodak invented digital photography** (1975). Engineers understood the technology and saw it would eventually replace film.

**But Kodak optimized the wrong system:** the film business.

**The feedback loop they didn't manage:**
- Digital cameras improve every year
- Quality approaches film film
- Price of digital cameras drops
- More people buy digital cameras (instead of film cameras)
- Fewer people buy film
- Film sales decline
- Kodak's revenue falls
- Kodak loses market share
- Kodak files for bankruptcy (2012)

**The irony:** Kodak invented the technology that destroyed them. They saw it coming but couldn't act.

## Why Systems Thinking Was Missing

Kodak optimized a single component: film profitability.

**What they failed to manage:**
- The imaging system was larger than film
- Digital cameras were not just a technology—they changed customer behaviour
- Customer behaviour created feedback loops (more digital adoption → less film demand)
- Those feedback loops were interconnected with business revenue
- Business revenue fed back into R&D investment
- Less R&D in film → competitive disadvantage → more sales losses

**The implicit strategy:** Invest in digital imaging research, but protect film profits. Milk film for cash while digital matured.

**The reality:** The market shifted faster than they could reposition. By the time they committed to digital, competitors (Canon, Nikon, Sony) had already won.

## The Deeper System

Kodak also failed to understand customer needs systemically:
- Film was not the product; **photography was**
- Customers wanted to capture and share images
- Digital enabled capturing, editing, and sharing from one device
- Film required darkrooms, chemical processing, and shipping
- The system shifted from "reliable storage" (film's value) to "instant sharing" (digital's value)

Kodak optimized film without asking: "What system is film part of? What happens when that system changes?"

## The Consequence

- Kodak went from market leader to bankruptcy
- Thousands of jobs lost
- Billions in value destroyed
- Missed the digital revolution entirely

## System Theory Insight

A technology doesn't exist in isolation. It's embedded in a system: customer behaviour, business models, competitive dynamics, feedback loops between adoption and profitability.

Local optimization (maximize film profits) can blind you to system-level change (digital adoption). When the system shifts, your optimization becomes a liability.

Systems thinking would have demanded:
- Understanding the imaging system as a whole (not just film)
- Recognizing feedback loops (adoption → demand → investment → competitive advantage)
- Accepting that the system would shift
- Preparing to lead the shift, not protect the old system
- Investing in new business models, not just new technology

**Key insight:** Sometimes the biggest systems thinking failure is optimizing the right component of the wrong system.
