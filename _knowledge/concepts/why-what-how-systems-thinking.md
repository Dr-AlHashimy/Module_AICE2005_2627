# The Why, What, and How of Systems Thinking

## Why Systems Thinking Exists

Systems thinking was developed to address three fundamental challenges:

### 1. Baloney

There is a lot of misinformation and false thinking around us. Systems thinking helps you avoid it by grounding thinking in interconnections and evidence rather than isolated claims.

### 2. Bias

Systems thinking helps us recognize our own biases. We often:
- See what we want to see
- See only things that support our argument
- Miss contradictory evidence

These thinking errors lead to unhappy outcomes. Systems thinking creates awareness of these patterns.

### 3. Reality: VUCA

The world is **Volatile, Uncertain, Complex, and Ambiguous**. This creates interconnected and wicked problems that cannot be solved by linear thinking. Systems thinking helps us deal with this by taking into account:
- Contextual factors
- Multiple perspectives
- Interconnections

## What Is Systems Thinking?

Systems thinking is **the act of organizing information to make meaning**. It means building mental models of things we experience in everyday life.

### The Six Blind Wise Men and the Elephant

Consider six blind wise men encountering an elephant for the first time. Each touches a different part:
- One touches the rope (tail) and thinks it's a rope
- One touches the tree (leg) and thinks it's a tree
- One touches the snake (trunk) and thinks it's a snake
- One touches the spear (tusk) and thinks it's a spear
- One touches the fan (ear) and thinks it's a fan
- One touches the mountain (body) and thinks it's a mountain

### The Lesson

**Each person sees something different based on:**
- Their locality (where they are positioned)
- The constraints of how they experience the system
- What part they can access

**No single person sees the elephant in its entirety.** Yet each is following a logical process based on their limited perspective.

This metaphor applies to all complex systems. Different people and interest groups see the same system differently, based on their position and experience. Understanding requires integrating multiple perspectives.

## How Do We Do Systems Thinking?

Systems thinking follows four simple steps for building understanding:

### Step 1: Distinguish Ideas from Other Ideas

Make distinctions. Separate one concept from another. Define what is part of the system and what is not.

### Step 2: Organize Ideas into Systems

Take those distinct ideas and organize them into a coherent system. Show how they relate to a whole.

### Step 3: Relate Ideas to One Another

Show the connections between ideas. Identify feedback loops, dependencies, and relationships.

### Step 4: Look at Ideas from Many Perspectives

Don't stop at your own view. Actively seek other perspectives—different positions, different expertise, different values. Ask what others see that you don't.

## Practice and Application

**The devil is in the details.** Knowing the four steps intellectually is not enough.

**The most important thing:** Practice applying these four steps to anything you're thinking about:
- Problems you need to solve
- Innovations or products you're creating
- Systems you're trying to understand

### Outcomes of Practice

Awareness of and practice with these four simple rules leads to:
- Better thinking
- Better outcomes
- Greater self-awareness
