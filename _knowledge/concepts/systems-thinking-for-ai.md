# Systems Thinking for AI Engineers

## Definition

Systems thinking is a theoretical and analytical lens for understanding complex systems by focusing on interactions, feedback loops, interdependencies, and emergent behaviour — in contrast to reductionism, which analyses each part in isolation. 

Applied to AI, it reframes AI systems as socio-technical ecosystems rather than isolated algorithms: an AI model's success depends on data flows, human and organisational context, ethics, and regulation, not just technical accuracy.

### Key concepts include 

- holism vs. reductionism, 
- interconnectedness, 
- system boundaries, 
- reinforcing/balancing feedback loops, 
- emergence, 
- hierarchy and nested systems, 
- delays and nonlinearity, and 
- adaptation/self-organisation (e.g. federated learning). 


### Continued 

Systemic design extends this into practice — designing AI ecosystems, not just algorithms, through participatory, stakeholder-inclusive methods using tools such as causal loop diagrams, giga-mapping, and stakeholder analysis matrices — and connects to distributed optimisation, where AI training pipelines (data preprocessing, training, inference, deployment) are decomposed into coordinated subsystems, mirroring distributed design of multi-component systems.
