# Use Cases and Personas

## Definition

Use cases and personas are a paired, evidence-based toolkit for capturing user-centred requirements: use cases describe *what* the system must do (actor–system interactions structured as name, actors, preconditions/triggers, main success scenario, alternative flows, and postconditions/business rules, often visualised at cloud/kite/sea levels of detail), while personas describe *who* matters (evidence-based archetypes built from research — interviews, observation, surveys — capturing identity, goals/motivations, behaviours/pain points, and technology context, and prioritised as primary, secondary, or supplementary). Mapping personas onto use cases validates requirement coverage, exposes conflicting needs, and supports scenario-based design, where narrative walkthroughs surface context, emotion, and constraints that pure specifications miss. Use cases excel at traceability and testability but underplay non-functional requirements; personas build empathy and prioritisation but risk stereotyping without rigorous validation — so both should be revisited and validated with real stakeholders throughout discovery, design, and deployment.
