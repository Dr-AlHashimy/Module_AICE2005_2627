# Systematic Design Principles

## Definition

Ten key principles operationalise systematic design into a repeatable practice: structured process orientation (a defined, repeatable sequence of design stages), problem decomposition (breaking the system into manageable subsystems), function-driven design (deriving solutions from desired functions), design space exploration and evaluation (generating and comparing multiple alternatives against objective criteria), interface definition and integration planning, requirements traceability (linking every decision back to a requirement), iterative refinement and feedback loops, documentation and knowledge management, design for lifecycle considerations (manufacturability, maintainability, sustainability from the start), and use of formal tools and models (CAD, SysML, simulation). A simple example is the paperclip: a single continuous piece of bent wire that achieves clear functionality, material efficiency, ease of manufacturing, and universal adaptability — demonstrating that systematic principles apply as much to a humble object as to a multi-component engineering system.
