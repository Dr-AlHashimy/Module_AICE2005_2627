# Systematic Design

## Definition

Systematic Design is a structured approach that decomposes systems into interacting components, applying design principles to manage intricate challenges effectively. 

1. Structured: Following repeatable processes with defined stages and checkpoints rather than intuitive approaches. This ensures consistency and quality across projects.

2. Decomposable: Breaking complex problems into manageable sub-problems that can be addressed independently, while maintaining awareness of interactions.

3. Iterative: Embracing feedback loops to continuously refine designs based on testing, simulation, and stakeholder input throughout the development lifecycle.

Systematic design is crucial for managing complexity, coordinating distributed teams, and ensuring that each subsystem contributes effectively to the global performance objectives. It is not simply a way to document a design process.  

## Key characteristics:

- Emphasises phased processes (e.g., requirements → architecture → implementation → testing)
- Often used in engineering and software development (e.g., V-model, waterfall models, MBSE)
- Supports modularisation, documentation, and project management
- Is about rigour and discipline in the design workflow

It stands in contrast to systemic design and systems thinking, which emphasise interconnections, feedback loops, and emergent behaviour in messy, socio-technical contexts rather than phased rigour, and to ad hoc design approaches, which rely on designer intuition and past experience without structured methodologies. 

In multi-component systems, systematic design becomes essential because complexity grows exponentially with each additional component, and it draws on the practical toolkit captured in "Systematic Design Principles" and the monolithic vs distributed design choice for how the resulting process is coordinated.

Where systematic design is process-oriented and phased, systemic design is relationship-oriented and participatory, making it the bridge between systems thinking (the analytical lens) and the structured discipline of systematic design (the delivery process).

It is a foundational approach that ensures: 

1. Reliability
2. Scalability
3. Integration

are establisahed across subsystems when applied to multi-component systems. It integrates concepts from systems __engineering, software, and product design.__

Systematic design stands in contrast to ad hoc design approaches, which rely heavily on designer intuition and past experience without structured methodologies.

In multi-component systems, systematic design becomes essential due to the exponential growth in complexity with each additional component.

## Key Principles of Systematic Design

| **Principle**                                     | **Description**                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Structured Process Orientation**                | Follow a defined, repeatable sequence of design stages to ensure clarity and consistency.          |
| **Problem Decomposition**                         | Break the system into manageable subsystems or components to reduce complexity.                    |
| **Function-Driven Design**                        | Begin with the desired functions and derive solutions that fulfill those functions.                |
| **Design Space Exploration and Evaluation**       | Generate multiple design alternatives and evaluate them using clear, objective criteria.           |
| **Interface Definition and Integration Planning** | Clearly define interfaces between components to ensure compatibility and successful integration.   |
| **Requirements Traceability**                     | Link every design decision back to a functional or non-functional requirement.                     |
| **Iterative Refinement and Feedback Loops**       | Use test results and stakeholder feedback to iteratively improve the design.                       |
| **Documentation and Knowledge Management**        | Maintain thorough records of decisions, risks, trade-offs, and changes throughout the process.     |
| **Design for Lifecycle Considerations**           | Consider manufacturability, maintainability, and sustainability from the start.                    |
| **Use of Formal Tools and Models**                | Apply CAD, SysML, simulation, and modelling tools to support rigour, precision, and communication. |
