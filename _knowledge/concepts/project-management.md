# Project Management

## Definition

Project management for multi-component systems orchestrates interacting subsystems and the people who build them, rather than tracking a single linear task list — hardware, software, and testing workstreams depend on each other and behave like an adaptive system with feedback loops and emergent risk. Core techniques include MoSCoW prioritisation (Must/Should/Could/Won't-have) to keep scope aligned with limited time and stakeholder expectations, T-shirt sizing (XS–XL) for fast, relative early-stage effort estimation before detailed scheduling is possible, Gantt charts for dependency-aware scheduling across teams, and burn-down charts to track remaining work against time — which also surface team overload or unsustainable pace early. Delivery approach must be chosen deliberately rather than defaulted to: Agile/Scrum suits fast- learning, software-heavy work; Stage-Gate/Waterfall suits certifiable artefacts and long-lead procurement; Hybrid ("Wagile") blends high-level gates with agile delivery inside them; and Critical Chain/Flow suits shared, capacity-constrained resources — each carries a systemic risk if applied where it doesn't fit (e.g. hardware and compliance teams left out of a software-only sprint cadence).
