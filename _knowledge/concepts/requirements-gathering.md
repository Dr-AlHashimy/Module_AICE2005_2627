# Requirements Gathering

## Definition

Requirements gathering (elicitation) is the systematic collection of stakeholder needs through multiple complementary techniques: interviews (structured or semi-structured), questionnaires/surveys, workshops and focus groups (card sorting, storyboarding, affinity mapping), ethnographic observation of real usage, document/system analysis of legacy systems and standards, low-fidelity prototyping, and user stories/use cases. Elicited requirements must then be validated for correctness, consistency, completeness, feasibility, and verifiability using techniques such as structured requirement reviews/walkthroughs, simulation and prototyping (e.g. FEA for hardware, wireframes for software), test-case derivation, traceability via a Requirements Traceability Matrix, feasibility checks, stakeholder review sessions, and formal requirement modelling (MBSE/SysML). In multi-component systems, requirements must be decomposed from system-level goals into component-level specifications, and conflicts between them are managed with prioritisation techniques such as MoSCoW and visualised using system modelling or giga-mapping.
